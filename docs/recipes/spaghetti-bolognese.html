Spaghetti Bolognese

A proper ragù in an hour: bacon fat, a hard crust on the mince, tomato purée cooked out, milk before the acid the Marcella Hazan way, then 20 minutes under pressure in the Ninja. The pasta is finished in the sauce with butter and Parmesan so it clings rather than sits on top.

Prep10 min
Cook55 min (20 min pressure)
Serves4
SourceHouse recipe

Method

  1. Ninja on sear/sauté high. Render the bacon in the oil until crisp and the fat has run, about 4 minutes. Lift the bacon out and leave the fat (or leave it in and carry on; it all melts into the sauce anyway).
  2. Add the mince, press it flat across the base and leave it alone for 4 minutes until it has a proper brown crust underneath. Then break it up and brown the rest, another 4 minutes. Most of the flavour comes from this step, so don't stir early.
  3. Add the onion, carrot and celery. Cook until soft, about 7 minutes. Add the garlic for the last minute (frozen cubes carry water, so they go in late).
  4. Stir in the tomato purée and cook it for 2 minutes until it darkens and smells sweet rather than tinny.
  5. Pour in the milk with the nutmeg and let it almost all cook away, about 5 minutes (the Marcella Hazan step, for silkiness).
  6. Add the wine if using and let it bubble off, 2 minutes.
  7. Return the bacon. Add the tomatoes, stock, Marmite (or soy or Worcestershire), bay, oregano and the Parmesan rind. Scrape the base clean so the Ninja doesn't throw a burn warning. Pepper now, hold the salt.
  8. Lock the lid. Pressure cook on high 20 minutes, then 10 minutes natural release and vent. Put the pasta water on the moment the lid locks. Or simmer on the hob, partially covered, 2 hours or more.
  9. Lid off. If the sauce is loose, sear/sauté uncovered for 5 minutes to thicken (a pressure cooker evaporates nothing, so it comes out as wet as it went in). Fish out the bay and rind. Now taste for salt: the bacon, Marmite and stock have already added plenty.
  10. Cook the spaghetti in well-salted water until one minute under al dente. Drain, keeping a mug of the water.
  11. Tip the pasta into the sauce with the butter, half the Parmesan and a good splash of pasta water. Toss hard for a minute until glossy and clinging; it should look slightly too loose, it tightens on the way to the table.
  12. Rest of the Parmesan on top. Better tomorrow, and freezes well, so make double if you can.
