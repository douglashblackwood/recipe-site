Pressure Cooker Chicken Enchiladas

Kenji López-Alt's dump-and-cook trick: the pressure cooker braises the chicken and makes the enchilada sauce in the same pot, then a quick assemble-and-bake. House version: chicken breast, big flour tortillas, no frying step, parmesan finish.

SourceSerious Eats
Serves4
Time~60 min

Ingredients

Method

  1. Snip the tops off the anchos, shake out the seeds, microwave 15 seconds until pliable and fragrant, snip into strips.
  2. Load the pressure cooker wet-veg first: tomatoes and onion at the bottom, chicken on top, then anchos, garlic, chipotles and adobo, bay, oregano, cumin, ground coriander, the Knorr water and a good grind of pepper. No salt yet.
  3. Pressure HIGH for 8 minutes, quick release. Check the breast is cooked through (72°C / no pink); a minute or two under the closed lid finishes a borderline piece.
  4. Lift the chicken out to cool. Discard the bay leaf. Blend everything left in the pot with the soy and lime until smooth. Taste, and salt only if it needs it. Stir in three-quarters of the coriander.
  5. Shred the breast with two forks and fold in about 4 tablespoons of sauce. Breast is lean, so be generous.
  6. Oven to 200°C (180°C fan). Ladle a good cup of sauce over the base of a roughly 20 × 30 cm dish.
  7. Spread the inside of each tortilla with a spoon of sauce, lay a quarter of the chicken down the middle, roll, and place seam-side down in the dish.
  8. Pour all remaining sauce over the top (flour tortillas drink it). Cheddar in two rows down the middle, then a few spoonfuls of sauce on top of the cheese: Kenji's trick for keeping it gooey.
  9. Foil on, bake 10 minutes. Foil off, half the parmesan on, 5-10 minutes more until bubbling.
  10. Finish with the remaining parmesan and coriander. Serve with any spare sauce at the table.
Heat map: the anchos are fruity, not hot; the chipotles are the real heat. One chipotle is mild and smoky, two is smoky-medium; adults correct at the table with hot sauce. Bail-out: stop after step 5 and you have excellent taco filling in ~35 minutes. The chicken and sauce also freeze well. Original: Kenji uses 900 g bone-in thighs at 15 minutes and 12 oil-fried corn tortillas; breast needs only 8 minutes (no fat or collagen to protect it, even in liquid).
