The BIR Curry System

A complete BIR method: a pressure-cooked two-component base, one house mix powder, a hard-and-fast fry, and a decoder for turning that single foundation into any curry on the menu.

Based on Al's Kitchen, Misty Ricardo, The Curry Guy (Dan Toombs), Glebe Kitchen & Kris Dhillon
The whole idea in one line Same base + same mix powder → a small, defined set of add-ons per curry. A restaurant plates fifteen curries fast because they are not cooking fifteen recipes, they are applying fifteen small "diffs" to two shared foundations. The catch: the diffs are few, so each one must be LOUD. A timid diff off a shared base tastes like the same curry again; v3 of this reference (28/07/2026) quantifies every diff at full takeaway volume.

1The base — two components, two jobs

The Al's Kitchen approach splits what most recipes lump together. Keep them separate, they do different work.

① The purée — the flavour base

Thick, concentrated blended onion + carrot. This is the bit you fry hard. Because it is a paste, not a watery gravy, it genuinely caramelises (the textbook "keep the base pale" rule is for the dilute one-pot gravy, not this one).

② The onion water — the liquid

The aromatic cooking liquid the onions and carrot pressure-cooked in. It replaces plain water or stock. Added hot, after the purée is fried, then boiled down to the final consistency.

Making the base (batch it — enough for 2 curries)

Into a pressure cooker: 1 kg onions (quartered), 1 medium carrot, 1 small piece cassia/cinnamon, 1 star anise, water just to cover. Pressure cook 12-15 min. Then separate:

Yield check: 1 kg of onions gives roughly 900 g purée (two curries at 450 g each) and a variable amount of onion water. If the onion water runs short of the ~400 ml per curry, top it up with hot water from the kettle, it is aromatic liquid, not a sacred stock.

  • Fish out the whole spices. Blend the solids (onion + carrot) to a thick smooth purée.
  • Keep the cooking liquid as the reserved onion water.
Why these tweaks Carrot = natural sweetness + the orange restaurant colour (so you can cut the added sugar). One medium carrot per kilo of onion, no more or it tastes of carrot. Star anise + onion is a savoury, almost meaty pairing (the pho trick), but it dominates fast, so one only. These live mostly in the base, so every curry inherits a faint warm-spice background, which is pleasant but means the base is "aromatic" rather than perfectly neutral.

The purée colour target

raw / palegoldendeep amber · russet ✓dark · bitter ✗
  • Heat: medium-high, good slug of oil, open pan (no lid, no cartouche, a cartouche steams, the opposite of what you want).
  • Look for: it tightens and darkens to deep amber / russet / light chestnut, and the oil splits out and pools. The oil breaking is the real "done" signal, more reliable than colour alone.
  • Smell: sweet, nutty, caramelised. Sharp or acrid = gone too far.
  • Time: 10-20 min. Eight minutes only softens, the depth comes from pushing past "looks cooked".
  • Rhythm: stir → let it catch → scrape the fond → repeat. Constant stirring prevents browning, walking away burns it.
  • The exception: the mild creamy curries (korma, pasanda) want a pale golden purée, not deep amber. Caramelised base would muddy their colour and fight the cardamom-coconut sweetness. Everything else on the menu gets the full hard fry.
Equipment note Use a pressure cooker / multi-cooker for the softening. But do the hard fry in a wide, heavy pan / karahi on the hottest hob ring. A multi-cooker's sauté is moderate and its deep narrow pot traps moisture, so it steams rather than fries. If staying one-pot: max sear, small batches, patience.

2The mix powder — the house blend

Make one jar, use it for everything. It replaces the per-recipe spice juggling. The building spice (fried early), as distinct from garam masala, the finishing spice (added late, off heat).

3Chicken tikka — the third foundation

The pre-cooked protein pillar. Cooked separately in a wood-fired pizza oven, the closest thing to a tandoor you can have at home, then dropped into any curry off the base. This is the house pre-cooked chicken: any recipe below that says "pre-cooked chicken" means this tikka (the char only adds).

The marinade — the house batch (650 g diced chicken breast = one curry)

  • 650 g diced chicken breast (one supermarket pack)
  • 65 g natural yoghurt (coconut yoghurt for dairy-free)
  • 1½ tsp garlic-ginger paste
  • Juice of ¼ lemon (a good squeeze)
  • 1 tbsp honey (scant)
  • A small splash of light/refined olive oil
  • 1½ tbsp tandoori masala powder
  • Orange food colouring (TRS brand), a few drops
  • 2½ tsp house mix powder (replaces loose coriander, cumin, turmeric & paprika — the jar is already made, use it)
  • ½ tsp Kashmiri chilli powder
  • Good pinch black pepper · ¼ tsp ground cardamom
  • 1 tsp kasuri methi · ¾ tsp dried mint
  • ¾ tsp salt (scant)

Method: mix everything, massage into the chicken, cover and refrigerate overnight, ideally 24 hours. Skewer and cook hot and fast (pizza oven technique below).

Batch maths: 650 g raw cooks down to roughly the 550 g pre-cooked chicken every serves-4 curry below calls for, so one batch = one curry, and it matches the standard 650 g breast pack. This is the batch actually cooked in this house (the original 1.5 kg party batch is everything × 2⅓, for a two-curry night with tikka starters).

Source: Al's Kitchen (YouTube), scaled to the house batch and converted to the pre-made mix powder (Al's loose coriander + cumin + turmeric + paprika sum to the same 2½ tsp; the jar's extra Madras powder and garam masala are welcome in a tandoori marinade). Tandoori masala does the heavy lifting; the methi and dried mint are the restaurant tells.

Why the pizza oven wins

Restaurant tikka is defined by the tandoor: 400-500°C, live-fire char, a kiss of woodsmoke. A domestic oven or grill can't touch it. A pizza oven can, so this is more authentic than most home cooks manage.

Technique

  • Raise it off the deck (skewers across a tray / a rack) so heat circulates and chars more than one face. Turn once or twice.
  • Blast and pull. Very hot, short time, char outside, juicy inside. Sitting long to "cook through" dries it out.
  • Keep the char. The blackened edges are the flavour you can't fake in the sauce.
Dairy-free note Al's tikka marinade is yoghurt-based, so as-is it is not dairy-free. For a dairy-free curry (the dairy-free korma or tikka masala), marinate in coconut / dairy-free yoghurt instead, it chars just the same. Otherwise the tikka is a drop-in for tikka masala, korma, makhani, a Saag Balti, or a standalone starter.

4The basic cook — step by step

The "mother" cook (a plain medium chicken curry, serves ~4). Branch it with the decoder in Part 5. Everything slow is front-loaded, so this live cook is a fast, hot 15 minutes.

House standards — read once, applies everywhere
  • Garlic-ginger paste: the Tesco frozen paste, straight from the freezer into the pan (it thaws in seconds at frying heat). "2 tbsp" in every recipe means 2 tbsp once melted, roughly 5-6 of the frozen portions.
  • Chilli: run TWO jars. The house jar is Kashmiri (mild, mostly colour); keep a second jar of hot Indian chilli powder for the top of the menu, because doubling Kashmiri stops working around "med-hot" and everything above it collapses into the same middling warmth — a big part of why curries converge. All heat quantities in the decoder and recipes below are given in house terms (Kashmiri and/or hot jar per dish), so no mental conversion at the hob. Fresh green chillies and Naga pickle are the other two heat levers.
  • Salt: 1 tsp per serves-4 curry, in with the onion water at Phase 3 (early salt seasons the sauce as it reduces). Taste at the end and adjust, remembering the sauce concentrates as it boils down.
  1. Phase 0 · ahead Have the mix powder jarred and the base made (purée + onion water separated). Pre-cook the chicken: in this house that means the Part 3 chicken tikka, marinated the day before and blasted in the pizza oven. Set aside. (Making ahead to reheat? Pull it slightly underdone.)
  2. Phase 1 · the Maillard fry Wide pan, hottest ring, 4 tbsp oil. Add ~400-500 g purée (about half the batch). Fry medium-high 10-15 min to deep amber, oil splitting. This is where the flavour is made.
  3. Phase 2 · bloom Splash more oil, fry 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste 1 min. Stir in 2-3 tbsp mix powder + 1 tbsp tomato purée loosened with a little water. Fry hard 60-90 sec, keep it moving so the powder doesn't catch.
  4. Phase 3 · build Add the per-curry differentiator now (basic version: ½-1 tsp chilli powder). Pour in ~400 ml onion water, hot (cold stalls the fry). Stir smooth. Add the pre-cooked chicken, 1 tsp salt + pinch of sugar.
  5. Phase 4 · reduce & finish Boil hard, lid off, 5-8 min to consistency. Off the heat: 1 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp kasoori methi (crushed in your palms), fresh coriander. Taste, adjust salt / sugar / acid.
  6. Phase 5 · serve or rest Serve now, or cool, rest overnight and reheat gently (low simmer, not another hard fry). Resting usually wins. Never slow-cook the finished curry.

5The decoder — one base, every curry

Add-ons are per single curry (serves ~4), on top of the basic cook. Heat is listed per dish in house-jar terms; the sauce target tells you how far to reduce.

Read this first — the three layers Every curry is built from three layers, not two. (1) Mix powder does ~80% of the spicing in all of them. (2) The finishing trio — garam masala + kasoori methi + fresh coriander — goes into nearly every savoury curry at the end (korma & pasanda being the mild exceptions, which lean on cardamom instead of methi). (3) The extras below are only what is different on top of those two shared layers. So a tikka masala is base + mix powder + the trio + the extras listed, not just almond and sugar.
Where the differences actually live — the four loud axes With 80% shared DNA, distinctness comes almost entirely from four axes, and each must be pushed to full volume: heat (the ladder from zero to Phal is wide — use the per-dish jar quantities, don't flinch), sweet–sour (a Pathia's tamarind and jaggery should both be unmissable, not detectable), consistency (a Bhuna is fried back to clinging-dry, a Madras pours — the onion-water volumes and reduction genuinely differ per dish, follow them), and body (coconut-almond cream vs tomato vs none). A curry that misses its axis targets tastes like "house curry, variant n" — which is the failure this v3 rewrite exists to fix.

Ordering like a takeaway — the flavour clusters Even at a real takeaway, neighbours converge: a Bhuna and a Madras share most of their DNA. The remembered "every curry tastes different" comes from ordering across clusters. When cooking two curries off one base batch, pick from two different clusters: Creamy-sweet (Korma, Pasanda, Tikka Masala) · Onion & texture (Dopiaza, Karahi, Jalfrezi) · Deep & dry (Bhuna) · Sweet-sour (Pathia, Dhansak) · Coconut-citrus (Ceylon) · Tomato-hot (Madras, Vindaloo, Naga, Phal) · Green (Saag). Korma + Jalfrezi are worlds apart; Bhuna + Madras are siblings. Choose accordingly.
The dairy-free route to a mild curry The mild curries (korma, pasanda, tikka masala) are exactly the ones blocked by cream, which is why a dairy-free repertoire tends to skew to the hotter non-creamy ones. But in BIR, korma's body comes from coconut + ground almond first, the dairy cream is just a top-up. Swap the cream for coconut cream + ground almond and you get a genuine dairy-free korma that is also mild and child-friendly, off the same base.

6Full recipes — every curry spelled out

Each is complete and standalone, serves ~4, built on the made-ahead base (purée + onion water), mix powder and pre-cooked protein. Bold = what makes this curry distinct, everything else is the shared spine. Method follows the 6-phase basic cook unless the note says otherwise.

Two tiers — why some have a "Dish spices" line and some don't Tier 1 (mix-powder-driven): Madras, Bhuna, Jalfrezi, Pathia, Phal honestly are spiced base + heat + the defining add. The base already carries cumin, coriander, turmeric, paprika, Madras powder & garam masala, so they don't need much more. That's real takeaway practice. Tier 2 (own spice signature): Korma, Dhansak, Saag, Karahi, Ceylon, Rogan Josh and Vindaloo have a genuine dish-specific blend that lifts them above "samey". Those carry a Dish spices line, bloom these with the mix powder at Phase 2 (or temper whole seeds in the oil first where noted). The Tier 1 corollary: since those dishes differ ONLY on heat, acid, sweetness and reduction, those four settings are the whole recipe — hit them at full volume or the dish collapses into "house curry again". Under-chilli a Madras and it is a Bhuna with lemon.

Mild

Korma Mild serves 4 · dairy-free

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g onion-carrot purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • ½ tsp ground cardamom · ¼ tsp ground cinnamon/cassia
  • no tomato, no chilli · 300 ml onion water (hot)
  • 3-4 tbsp ground almonds · 75 g creamed coconut · 2-3 tsp sugar — properly sweet, that is the dish
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: ½ tsp garam masala · fresh coriander (no methi)

Keep it pale, go easy on the purée colour. Stir in almond + coconut + sugar and simmer gently. Coconut-led, unapologetically sweet, no heat. Sauce: creamy and gently loose. (vs pasanda: korma is the sweet coconut one.)

Pasanda Mild serves 4 · dairy-free

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • ¼ tsp ground cardamom + a pinch of nutmeg
  • no chilli · 300 ml onion water (hot)
  • 5 tbsp ground almonds (nut-led — the point of the dish) · 100 ml coconut cream · 1 tsp sugar only
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken (traditionally lamb) · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: ½ tsp garam masala · coriander · toasted flaked almonds on top (no methi)

Not "korma again": almond-rich and barely sweetened where korma is coconut-sweet. Sauce: thick, spoon-coating. The flaked-almond top is the tell.

Tikka Masala Mild serves 4 · dairy-free

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 1-2 tsp Kashmiri chilli / paprika
  • 200 g passata + 1 tbsp tomato purée · 350 ml onion water (hot)
  • 100 ml coconut cream · 1 tbsp ground almond · 2 tsp sugar
  • 550 g tandoori chicken tikka, properly charred (the smoke is a main ingredient) · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1.5 tsp kasoori methi · coriander

Bloom chilli + mix powder + passata, then add cream + almond + sugar + methi. Drop in the tikka last. Red, creamy, lightly sweet, smoky. Sauce: rich and coating.

Dopiaza Mild serves 4

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • ½-1 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 1 tbsp tomato purée + 100 g chopped tomato · only 350 ml onion water
  • 2 onions, two ways: one in chunky pieces fried till caramelised at the edges, folded in near the end · petals of the second in at the reduction, so they keep bite
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt · pinch sugar
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

The "two onions" dish means onion is the texture AND the sweetness: soft caramelised chunks plus just-tender petals in every forkful. If it eats like a plain curry with some onion in it, the dose was too low. Sauce: medium-thick, crowded with onion.

Medium

Bhuna Medium serves 4

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 3 tbsp mix powder (extra)
  • 2-3 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 2 tbsp tomato purée + 100 g chopped tomato
  • only 200 ml onion water (hot) — then fry the sauce BACK: keep cooking past "reduced" until the oil re-splits and the sauce clings to the meat
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt · pinch sugar
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 2 tsp methi · coriander

The bhuna IS the fry-back: almost-dry, spice-crusted, deepest of the medium curries. If it pours off a spoon it is not done yet. The consistency gap between this and a Madras should be unmissable.

Dhansak Medium serves 4 · sweet & sour

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 2-3 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 1 tbsp tomato purée · 400 ml onion water (hot)
  • Dish spices: 1 tsp toasted ground cumin · ¼ tsp cinnamon · pinch ground clove · pinch ground cardamom · ¼ tsp ground fenugreek
  • 250 g cooked red lentils (masoor) — they are the body of the sauce, not a garnish · 1 tbsp sugar · 2 tbsp lemon juice (pineapple chunks are classic BIR, not a gimmick)
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Thick and porridgey from the lentils, and the sweet-sour must be LOUD: taste for all three poles (sweet / sour / hot) by name before serving. If none jumps out, add more of all three, not none.

Saag Medium serves 4 · Balti style

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 1 tbsp tomato purée · 350 ml onion water (hot)
  • Dish spices: 1 tsp whole cumin seed (tempered in the oil first) · 2 extra garlic cloves · 1-2 green chillies
  • 300 g spinach (fresh or defrosted frozen) — the curry should be visibly green, not flecked · 2 tbsp mango chutney near the end (the balti lift) (optional 1 tbsp coconut cream to finish)
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken or tikka · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Temper the whole cumin in the oil before the purée. Stir the spinach through near the end. With tikka this is a Saag Balti.

Karahi Medium serves 4 · dry-ish

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 2-3 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 2 tbsp tomato purée + 100 g chopped tomato
  • Dish spices: 2 tsp coarsely crushed coriander seed (the signature — crush, don't grind) · 1 tsp cumin (part whole) · ½ tsp crushed black pepper
  • ½ green pepper + ½ onion in chunks · only 250 ml onion water (hot)
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1.5 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander · julienned ginger on top

Keep it dry-ish and fresh, garam-masala forward. Scatter raw ginger to serve.

Vegetable Curry Mild-med serves 4 · vegan

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • ½-1 tsp chilli powder (drop to zero for the kids) · 1 tbsp tomato purée + 100 g chopped tomato · 350 ml onion water (hot)
  • 1 large potato, diced & par-boiled · ½ small cauliflower in florets, par-boiled · 100 g frozen peas · ½ pepper in chunks (optional: 1 tin chickpeas, drained)
  • 1 tsp salt · pinch sugar
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Par-boil the potato and cauliflower ahead (they are the "pre-cooked protein" of this dish) so the live cook stays a fast 15 minutes. Peas and pepper go in raw at the reduction. Works as a main or a side to any curry above.

Rogan Josh Medium serves 4

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 2 tsp Kashmiri chilli (colour first) · 2 tbsp tomato purée + 200 g chopped tomato · ½ red pepper — tomato-led, deep red
  • Dish spices: ¾ tsp ground fennel (the aromatic tell) · 2 cloves · small piece cinnamon · 2 green cardamom · 1 bay leaf
  • 350 ml onion water (hot)
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken or lamb · 1 tsp salt · pinch sugar
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · tomato wedges + coriander garnish

Tomato and red-pepper led, deep red. Garnish with fresh tomato and coriander.

Pathia Med-hot serves 4 · hot-sweet-sour

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 3-4 tsp Kashmiri chilli + 2 extra garlic cloves · 1 tbsp tomato purée · only 300 ml onion water
  • 2½-3 tbsp tamarind paste · 1½-2 tbsp jaggery / sugar — the sweet AND the sour must both be unmissable, or it isn't a pathia
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken or prawn · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Hot, sweet, sour, in that order of loudness, on a tight sticky-glossy sauce. The old 1-tbsp-tamarind version was detectable-not-unmissable, which made it taste like "house curry with a squeeze of something": push both poles until they announce themselves.

Hot & extreme

Jalfrezi Med-hot serves 4

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • ½ tbsp tandoori masala · 2-3 tsp Kashmiri chilli · 2-3 green chillies (sliced) — the fresh chillies carry the heat, don't skip them
  • ½ green + ½ red pepper + ½ onion in chunks (charred if you like) · 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • only 300 ml onion water (hot) — keep it dry
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt · pinch sugar
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Stir-fry style, dry and punchy. Char the peppers and onion for the signature bite.

Ceylon Hot serves 4 · dairy-free

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2 tbsp mix powder
  • 2 tsp HOT chilli powder (or 4 tsp Kashmiri + 2 green chillies) · 1 tbsp tomato purée · only 250 ml onion water (the coconut milk is the rest of the liquid)
  • Dish spices: swap 1 tbsp of the mix powder for 1-2 tsp roasted Ceylon/Sri Lankan curry powder · a sprig of curry leaves, tempered in the oil first
  • 200 ml coconut milk · juice and zest of 1 lime
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Coconut + lime + heat, all three at full volume: the wettest, brightest hot curry on the menu, nearly a Sri Lankan hodi. If it just tastes "hot", the coconut and lime were too timid.

Madras Hot serves 4

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2-3 tbsp mix powder
  • 2-3 tsp HOT chilli powder (or 4-6 tsp Kashmiri + 2 green chillies) · 2 tbsp tomato purée + 100 g chopped tomato
  • 400 ml onion water (hot) · 1 tbsp lemon juice · fry the purée a shade darker than usual
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Hot and tangy, tomato-forward, pourable. The benchmark "big kick" curry: this is the dish the second (hot) jar exists for. Kashmiri-only at doubled volume still lands closer to medium, which is how a Madras ends up tasting like the Bhuna.

Vindaloo Very hot serves 4 · very hot

  • 4 tbsp oil · 450 g purée · 2 tbsp garlic-ginger paste · 2-3 tbsp mix powder
  • 3-4 tsp HOT chilli powder + 2 tsp Kashmiri · ½ tsp black pepper · 4-6 extra garlic cloves
  • Dish spices: pinch each ground clove, cinnamon & cumin · ½ tsp mustard seed (the Goan warm-spice backbone)
  • 2 tbsp tomato purée · 400 ml onion water (hot)
  • 3 tbsp vinegar, in EARLY with the onion water so it cooks into the sauce rather than sitting on top · 1 potato, diced & pre-cooked
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken (or pork, traditional) · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Vinegar-garlic first, heat second: a vindaloo you could not identify blindfolded by its sharpness alone is just a hotter Madras. The garlic dose is deliberate.

Phal Extreme serves 4 · novelty heat

  • Build exactly as Madras, then go to the limit:
  • 4-6 tsp hot chilli powder · 2-3 fresh scotch bonnet / naga · optional 1 tbsp naga pickle
  • 550 g pre-cooked chicken · 1 tsp salt
  • Finish: 1 tsp garam masala · 1 tsp methi · coriander

Heat for heat's sake. Ventilate the kitchen, handle chillies with care.

Naga pickle — the secret weapon (works across the menu) Beyond its own dish, ¼-1 tsp of Naga pickle stirred into a Jalfrezi, Madras, Bhuna or Pathia at the reduction stage adds heat plus tang, garlic and savoury depth that dry chilli powder can't. A flavour move, not just a heat move, this is what Al's Kitchen is doing. Ferociously concentrated, so go small and build, balance with a pinch of sugar. Leave it out of any mild portions. 7Technique rules to live by

The two fries are different

Don't conflate them. The purée fries dark (that's the Maillard). The finished sauce is built fast and bright on top. Burnt purée is bitter and unrecoverable, stop when the oil splits.

Hard and fast, not slow

BIR is engineered for a 5-10 min final cook. Slow-cooking the assembled curry dulls the fried-spice hit and dries the chicken. Slow cooking is for traditional from-scratch curries, a different method.

Rest beats instant

Cook hard, then cool, rest overnight, reheat gently. Fat-soluble spices keep infusing and harshness mellows. If reheating, pull the chicken slightly underdone first so it doesn't toughen.

Mix powder vs garam masala

Mix powder goes in early and gets fried (building). Garam masala goes in late, off the heat (finishing). Keeping these two roles separate is the bit most home cooks miss.
